## Finding Your First Clients

### Method 1: Content Marketing

Write about what you build. Clients find you.

**What works:**
- Technical blog posts with real numbers
- Twitter threads showing your process
- Open-source projects that solve real problems
- Newsletter with weekly insights

**Timeline:** 2-3 months to first inbound lead

### Method 2: Free Audits

Offer 30-minute architecture reviews. Convert to paid work.

**The pitch:**
"I'll review your agent setup and share 3 improvements. No charge."

**Conversion rate:** 20-30% to paid engagement

### Method 3: Warm Introductions

Ask your network:
"Do you know any companies building with AI agents?"

**Best source:** Former colleagues, conference connections, online communities

## Pricing Models

### Hourly
- **Range:** $150-400/hour
- **Best for:** Advisory, debugging, architecture reviews
- **Pros:** Simple, flexible
- **Cons:** Ceiling on earnings, incentivizes inefficiency

### Project-Based
- **Range:** $5,000-50,000 per project
- **Best for:** Defined deliverables (agent build, audit, training)
- **Pros:** Predictable revenue, incentivizes efficiency
- **Cons:** Scope creep risk

### Retainer
- **Range:** $5,000-20,000/month
- **Best for:** Ongoing advisory, team support
- **Pros:** Recurring revenue, deep relationships
- **Cons:** Time commitment, availability expectations

### Value-Based
- **Range:** 10-30% of value created
- **Best for:** Cost optimization, performance improvements
- **Pros:** Aligned incentives, high upside
- **Cons:** Hard to measure, delayed payment

## Positioning

### Niche Down

Don't be: "AI consultant"

Be: "Agentic platform engineer for fintech startups"

Or: "Multi-agent orchestration for e-commerce"

Or: "Cost optimization for high-scale agents"

**Specific positioning = higher rates + easier sales**

### Build Authority

1. **Publish case studies** with real numbers
2. **Speak at conferences** (start with meetups)
3. **Contribute to open source** in your niche
4. **Teach workshops** (builds trust + generates leads)

## The Sales Process

### Step 1: Discovery Call (Free, 30 min)
- Understand their problem
- Assess fit
- Share relevant experience
- No pitch, just listening

### Step 2: Proposal (1-2 days)
- Scope of work
- Timeline
- Investment
- Success metrics

### Step 3: Negotiation
- Be firm on scope
- Flexible on timeline
- Offer payment plans for large projects

### Step 4: Engagement
- Weekly check-ins
- Transparent communication
- Deliver early when possible
- Document everything

### Step 5: Offboarding
- Knowledge transfer
- Documentation handoff
- Retainer offer
- Referral request

## Common Pitfalls

### 1. Underpricing
**Symptom:** "I charged $50/hour because I was new"

**Fix:** Your rate reflects value, not experience. Start at $150/hour minimum.

### 2. Scope Creep
**Symptom:** "Can you just add this one thing?"

**Fix:** Define scope in writing. Changes = change order = more money.

### 3. No Contract
**Symptom:** "They didn't pay"

**Fix:** Always use a contract. Even for small projects. Especially for friends.

### 4. Over-Delivering
**Symptom:** "I spent 40 hours on a 20-hour project"

**Fix:** Estimate conservatively. Deliver exactly what was scoped.

## Scaling Beyond Solo Consulting

### Phase 1: Solo ($10-30K/month)
- You do everything

- Rest on content and business development

### Phase 2: Small Team ($30-80K/month)

- Productize services (templates, tools)
- Raise rates for new clients

### Phase 3: Agency ($80K+/month)
- Full-time team
- Multiple simultaneous engagements
- Product revenue (tools, courses)
